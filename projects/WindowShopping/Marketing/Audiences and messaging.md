# Audiences and messaging

## Primary audience: intentional online shoppers

They browse across multiple stores, save purchases for later, and dislike losing products in tabs or screenshots. They may be waiting for a sale, payday, a gift occasion, or a restock.

Message: "Keep every find in one place, then decide when it is worth buying."

Best proof: show a messy browser state becoming a named collection with product images, notes, and prices.

## Secondary audience: style, home, and hobby planners

They build collections around a future look, room, trip, or project. A product is useful even when they have no immediate plan to purchase it.

Message: "Make a wishlist that is actually useful when you are ready."

Best proof: demonstrate collections such as Apartment ideas, Wedding guest, Running gear, or Gifts.

## Secondary audience: budget-conscious waiters

They have seen an item they want but prefer to wait for a lower price or a target price. They value clear information more than manufactured urgency.

Message: "Save the item, set your plan, and let a supported price check do the watching."

Best proof: explain that supported products are checked about hourly while the browser and device are available, with price changes confirmed by two successful observations.

## Secondary audience: gift planners

They collect ideas throughout the year and need a simple way to remember an item, its source, and the intended recipient.

Message: "Save gift ideas when you find them, not when you can remember them later."

Best proof: a collection grouped by person or occasion with a note for size, color, or context.

## Objections and responses

| Objection | Honest response |
|---|---|
| "I already use tabs." | Tabs are good for the moment. Collections keep a find available when the moment has passed. |
| "Will it work with every store?" | You can save from almost any supported HTTPS store. Automatic monitoring depends on retailer support and product data. |
| "Will I get an instant alert?" | Supported products are checked about hourly while the browser and device are available. A price change needs two successful observations before an alert. |
| "Do I need an account?" | No. An account is optional and lets users back up and view saves across devices. |
| "What happens to my data?" | WindowShopping has no ads and does not sell user data. |

## Content pillars

1. Save now, decide later: relief from lost finds and impulse-buying pressure.
2. Shopping organization: collections, notes, tags, and a clearer wishlist.
3. Patient deal watching: how qualified price and restock alerts work.
4. Seasonal planning: gifts, travel, home, wardrobe, and hobby projects.
5. Practical trust: free to use, no ads, optional account sync, and clear limitations.
